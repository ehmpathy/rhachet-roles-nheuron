## the handoff — what only a human can do

deliverable (c), the demonstration, cannot proceed from here. each step below needs a console
session or a credential this robot must not hold.

| # | step | why it is human-only |
|---|------|----------------------|
| 1 | create `nheuron-root`, then its aws organization | declastruct cannot mint the org it authenticates as. see `provision/aws.auth/account=.root/bootstrap.md` |
| 2 | fill `NHEURON_PROD_ACCOUNT_EMAIL` + `NHEURON_PREP_ACCOUNT_EMAIL` | each is a fresh, unused address a human owns. **the two must differ** — the wish refuses to import otherwise (defect 26), since aws would take the org and one account before it refused the second |
| 3 | apply the account wish **as the root** | needs an `AWS_PROFILE` for `nheuron-root` |
| 4 | apply the mail wish **into prep** | needs an `AWS_PROFILE` for `nheuron-prep` |
| 5 | fill `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` + `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` | a cloudflare **user** token, minted by hand |
| 6 | fill `SES_DKIM_TOKENS` (the 3 tokens, comma-separated), apply the dns wish | the tokens exist only after step 4 |
| 7 | send a test mail to `jelly@one.nheuron.com`, then gather it | the live loop |
| 8 | fill `GMAIL_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON` + `_SUBJECT`, then run `TEST_LIVE=gmail` | the live gmail `[case7]` — see § verification and **F8** |

the runnable, ordered form of these steps: `blocker/account-setup.md`. none gates the merge.

steps 4→6 have a **hard order**: the ses identity emits the dkim tokens the dns wish declares.
the dns wish fails loud while `SES_DKIM_TOKENS` is empty, so the order cannot be gotten wrong
silently.

⚠️ step 6 is a **keyrack fill, not a source edit** — the tokens read from the sourced env, so
the committed dns wish is applyable as-is with no code change between the two applies (peer
r008 blocker.1 / r009 blocker.4). step 8 is independent of steps 1–7 and can be done at any time.

### who assumes `nheuron-mail-clone`

the trust policy names the account principal (`arn:aws:iam::<nheuron-prep>:root`), so any
principal in `nheuron-prep` whose own iam policy allows it may assume the role. the clone runs
`rhx gather.mail` locally under `AWS_PROFILE`, never on an instance, so an ec2 trust would leave
the role reachable by no one. `resources.mail.integration.test.ts` `[t2b]` clamps it.

### the questions the rounds converged on

> the self-reviews found defects of **correctness** — a bad lifecycle, a stale cast, a dead
> parameter. the peer round found defects of **reachability**: a deliverable fully coded and
> un-runnable (32), a flag fully parsed and discarded (31), a case fully authored and skipped
> (33), a hint fully worded and wrong (35).
>
> then a third class, one step worse than either: **computed, persisted, documented — and read
> by no one.** the sync cursor (44), the token parser (47), and the prefix contract (48) were
> each a piece of real, correct, live machinery wired to no consumer, with a doc-comment that
> described the behavior a reader would assume. a field with no reader is not merely dead; it is
> a promise the code cannot keep.
>
> and a fourth, which the repair of the third produced (51). the cursor is correct. it is
> reachable. it now HAS a reader — I gave it one, and that is the defect. `mailFoundUptil`
> claims *"every mail up to T has been seen"*; a filtered fetch backs only *"every mail up to T
> that matched"*, and the field could not tell them apart because its key could not. **a value
> whose claim outruns its evidence is worse than a value with no reader** — the dead field was
> inert, and the wired one lied.
>
> and a fifth, which only shows under load (61, 62). the ses page walk and the cache body read
> were each correct, reachable, read, and honest about their claim — and each would fail only
> once a mailbox was large. **a fault that hides until the system is busy meets its reader at
> the worst possible moment.** the second is the sharper: I had written the rule against that
> exact shape on the WRITE half, in the same directory, one iteration earlier, and left the READ
> half unguarded. **a rule you have written is not a rule you have applied.**
>
> ⇒ five questions, not one:
> **is this correct?** · **can this be run, as committed?** · **who reads what it produces?** ·
> **what does it CLAIM, and did the run that produced it earn the claim?** ·
> **what happens to this when it is BUSY?**
>
> ⚠️ the fourth is also the clearest lesson about repair itself: **the fix to a dead field is the
> moment its claim first matters.** two iterations of review passed between the wire-up and the
> catch.
>
> ⚠️ and the fifth carries the lesson about **proof**: a clamp you have not watched fail is a
> guess. the probe for defect 62's bound measured a peak of 0 and passed — it proved the
> monkeypatch, not the code. the discipline that caught it is the one that catches all of these:
> land the fix, revert it, and require the test to go RED before you believe it.
