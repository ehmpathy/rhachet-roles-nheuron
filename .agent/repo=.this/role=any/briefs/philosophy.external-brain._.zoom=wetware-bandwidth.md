# philosophy: the external brain — zoom: wetware bandwidth

> a zoom-in on `philosophy.external-brain`. the parent states *what* an external brain is;
> this one zooms on the **seam** between it and the wetware, and on why bandwidth across that
> seam is the constraint that binds.

## .it emanates from the wetware

the external brain is not a separate mind you consult. it **emanates from your wetware** — an
outward continuation of the same cognition, not a second party to it.

this distinction decides every choice downstream:

| a tool you consult | a brain that emanates |
|--------------------|-----------------------|
| you formulate a query, it answers | it already holds your context |
| separate mind, separate goals | one cognition, extended outward |
| you translate intent into a prompt | intent flows without translation |
| the boundary is a wall | the boundary is a gradient |
| success = good answers | success = the seam disappears |

so the design target is not "smarter answers." it is **maximum bandwidth** across the seam
between wetware and silicon. a brilliant external brain you can only reach through a keyhole
is worth less than a modest one wired wide.

**bandwidth is the constraint that binds** — not storage, not model quality. read every design
choice here as: does this widen the channel, or narrow it?

- capture from wherever the thought already drifts (do not make a human re-enter it)
- a clone with its own address, so the brain can reach back unprompted
- persistent context, so no session begins with re-explanation
- the human's own vocabulary, so intent needs no translation layer

each is a bandwidth argument. re-entry, re-explanation, and translation are all tolls charged
at the seam. the ideal is that they cost approximately none.

## .the accelerando benchmark

> think Manfred Macx's wearable in *Accelerando* — an early version of this.

Stross's Manfred wanders with augmented-reality spex and a swarm of agents that filter,
correlate, and negotiate on his behalf. his cognition genuinely extends through them.

the scene that names the stakes: when the spex are taken from him, he is not merely
inconvenienced — he is **functionally lobotomized**. the loss reads as brain damage rather than
a lost device, because the augment had become continuous with the mind instead of adjacent to it.

that is the benchmark, and it cuts two ways:

- **the aspiration** — an external brain of bandwidth high enough that it is genuinely part of
  your cognition. Macx's spex are an early version; so is this.
- **the caution** — continuity that deep makes the substrate carry real weight. so it must be
  *yours*: portable, inspectable, forkable, and free of any party who can take it away. this is
  why the brain is plain files and open roles rather than someone else's service.

we are early. today's channel is text, terminals, and mailboxes — not spex. but the direction
is set: every increment should widen the seam, toward the point where the augment no longer
reads as separate at all.

## .how to apply it

when you weigh a design choice, ask the bandwidth question before the capability question:

| the toll it charges | how to widen the seam |
|---------------------|-----------------------|
| a human must re-enter a thought that already exists elsewhere | collect it from where it already drifts |
| a session opens with re-explanation of context | persist the context |
| intent must be translated into the tool's vocabulary | adopt the human's vocabulary |
| the brain can only ever answer, never initiate | give it an address so it can reach back |

a feature that adds capability while it charges a new toll at the seam is usually a net loss.
prefer the modest capability that flows without friction.
